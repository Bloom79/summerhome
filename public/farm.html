Why B2B-first

Wholesale fills the baseline at £8/kg with one relationship; pre-order and dried lift the blended price without a shop counter. Fresh local mushrooms have a 3-day shelf life — that is the moat: no import competes, and no AI delivers a lion's mane picked this morning.

Post-mortem

Why they failed — and the countermeasure built into this plan

Container and vertical farms have been dying since 2023 — including a mushroom company. Each card is a real corpse, what actually killed it, and the concrete counter this plan takes. Not slogans: dials, layout rules and tripwires below.

Freight Farms · 2025

Sold $150k containers to buyers who couldn't profit. Their own numbers needed 15–25 h/wk of customer labour to beat supermarket lettuce; the market of "people who want a farm" outgrew "people who can run one profitably". Growth stalled, SPAC died, Chapter 7 with $7M liabilities. → Here: nothing is sold to anyone until your own logged P&L proves the buyer's economics — and the ticket is a £400 controller, not a $150k box.

Farmhand cloud · 2025

When the company died, every customer's farm went dark. Cloud dosing, climate and history vanished overnight; spare parts stopped; growers now swap fixes on Reddit. → Here: the controller is local-first — on-device AI, works offline forever, standard parts (Pi, relays, off-the-shelf sensors), no subscription needed to keep growing. Thousands of orphaned Freight Farmers are the demonstrated market for exactly this.

Smallhold · 2024

Mushrooms didn't save them. VC-scale facilities + national retail at wholesale prices = commodity revenue on artisan costs: COGS ×3 in a year, −$14.7M. They re-emerged as a partner-farm network — decentralised production under one brand. → Here: stay owner-scale on rented ground; if this ever scales, it scales the Smallhold-2.0 way (other farms run your spec and controller), never by building a facility.

Energy wall · 2023–25

Electricity was 40–60% of opex for the lettuce cohort (Bowery £700M burned, AeroFarms, AppHarvest, Jones Food UK). Viability needs 11–15 kWh/kg; most ran worse. → Here: fungi need ~2.2 kWh/kg and no grow lights — but it's one factor, not absolution: the itemised cost chart above keeps the electricity share visible so drift gets caught.

Micro-farm burnout

Small mushroom farms rarely go bankrupt — the owner breaks. Harvest windows are half a day; contamination spreads when flow runs dirty→clean; substrate supply bottlenecks. → Here: one-way clean→dirty layout from day 1, staggered batches, and the local-AI cameras below turn the crop's calendar into phone alerts instead of constant presence.

Price erosion

Oyster growing has low barriers — everyone watches the same courses, local gluts crash prices, and Edinburgh already has Rhyze, Some Funguys, Mycobee. → Here: blended-price tripwire below; lion's mane weighted in the mix (harder to grow = higher barrier, better price); the dried/kit channel absorbs any glut instead of dumping it fresh.

The local-AI edge — 2026, not sci-fi

Two cameras per fruiting room + a small vision model running on the Pi itself (edge models handle this today): detects pinning, flags harvest-ready blocks within the half-day window, and catches green mould at first pixel — the two things that actually kill micro-farms. All offline, no cloud to die, ~£120 of hardware already in the fit-out budget. This is the moat: any grower can buy a humidifier; almost none can build this — and it's the product you eventually sell to the Freight Farms orphans.

Tripwires

When to change course — decided now, not in a panic

cash < £1,000

Stop buying, sell down. If the bank line touches £1,000, freeze all kit purchases and run harvest-to-order only. The model shows your current floor in the first tile above.

contamination > 15%

Process problem — stop scaling. Green mould above 15% of blocks means the pasteurisation or air handling is wrong. Fix the process on small batches before inoculating another full run; the controller logs tell you which variable drifted.

wholesale uptake < 50% @ wk 12

Shift the mix. If the wholesaler takes less than half your volume, push the dried/kit share up (the dials above show what that does to the blended price) and add a second pre-order platform before growing more.

no power on the yard

Walk away. A generator adds ~£40–60/week and kills the margin. Only take ground with a mains feed — there are more yards every week (8 live within 30 mi today).

blended price < £9/kg for 4 wks

Local glut — stop selling fresh into it. Shift the dials: dried/kit share up, lion's mane share up, fresh oyster volume down. The blended-price line in Step 2 shows the current number; below £9 the fresh channel is subsidising someone else's hobby.

2 weeks > 25 h

Burnout is a failure mode, not a virtue. If two consecutive weeks pass 25 hours, cut fresh volume 30% and let the cameras and staggered batches catch up — the plan only works if it still works in month 10.

🔧 Montaggio

1
Posizione

Cantina, garage o stanza a nord: serve 14-20°C il più possibile stabile, niente sole diretto, presa elettrica, pavimento lavabile. La tenda contiene l'umidità: sotto metti un telo.

2
Aria e acqua

Ventola di estrazione in alto (fori esistenti della tenda), intake passivo in basso col filtro. Umidificatore fuori dalla tenda col tubo dentro, o dentro sul fondo. La nebbia NON deve bagnare i blocchi direttamente.

3
Sensori e camera

SHT31+SCD40 a metà altezza, lontani dal getto dell'umidificatore. Camera frontale che vede tutti i blocchi, LED 12h/giorno. Cablaggio fuori dalla zona umida, connettori sigillati.

4
Loop di controllo

Regole: RH 85-92% (isteresi 3%), CO₂ < 800-1000 ppm in fruttificazione (la ventola parte a soglia), cicli FAE ogni 2-4h comunque. Alert Telegram su: RH fuori range >30 min, CO₂ alto, sensore muto. Log su file: è il dataset.

5
Vision

Foto ogni 30 min. Modello locale (anche un classificatore semplice all'inizio): pinning sì/no, pin→raccolta (conta i giorni), verde=muffa→alert immediato. La finestra di raccolta è mezza giornata: questo è il pezzo che vale.

🔧 Montaggio

1
Camera

Svuota il frigo, togli il compressore se vuoi (o lascialo spento), fora un passacavo in basso e uno sfiato piccolo in alto (col filtro a carboni). Sigilla col silicone.

2
Riscaldamento

Tappetino sul fondo, griglia sopra (l'aglio non tocca il calore diretto), sonda al centro TRA le teste, non in aria libera. Taratura: overshoot <2°C — con SSR e PID su Pi ci arrivi in una serata.

3
Umidità e carico

Ciotola d'acqua sul fondo. Teste INTERE, non sbucciate. Test in parallelo: metà avvolte singolarmente (foil/carta), metà sfuse in barattolo aperto — annota quale viene meglio.

4
Software

Log continuo T/RH/kWh, alert se la temperatura esce di ±3°C, foto settimanale della testa campione. Profili programmabili: è quello che i produttori col rice cooker non hanno.
